Solar panels make sense for many UK homes, but the payback varies a lot. Start with the [solar calculator](/#solar) to get a rough idea, then work through the questions below.

## Your roof

- **Direction:** south-facing roofs produce the most. East and west still work well, especially if you use power in the morning or evening. North-facing roofs rarely pay off.
- **Shade:** chimneys, trees and neighbouring buildings can cut output sharply. Ask the installer to show you a shading assessment.
- **Condition:** panels last 25 years or more. If the roof needs work soon, do that first.
- **Permission:** most homes don't need planning permission, but listed buildings and conservation areas often do. Check with your council.

## Your electricity use

Solar saves the most when you use the power yourself, because every unit you use saves the full import price, while exported units earn less. If you're out all day, a battery or timing your appliances helps.

## The money

- **VAT:** solar panels and batteries for homes currently have 0% VAT. That is due to run until 31 March 2027, after which it's expected to return to 5%.
- **Export payments:** under the Smart Export Guarantee, suppliers pay you for electricity you send to the grid. Rates vary widely between suppliers, so compare them. To qualify, you'll need an MCS-certified installation and a smart meter that records exports.
- **Quotes:** get at least three. Compare the price per kWp, the panel and inverter brands, the warranties, and what's included (scaffolding, bird protection, monitoring).

## The installer

- Choose an **MCS-certified** installer. It protects you and is needed for export payments.
- Check they belong to a consumer code, such as RECC or HIES, that gives you a route to complain.
- Be wary of high-pressure selling, "today only" prices, or anyone who won't put the expected output in writing.

## Reading the calculator's answer

The calculator gives an estimate based on your region and typical patterns. Panels usually last 25 years or more, so a payback well inside that is a good sign. If the payback is long, try a smaller system, a better export rate, or the same system without a battery, and see which changes it most.

Rules and rates change, so check the current position on gov.uk before you buy.
