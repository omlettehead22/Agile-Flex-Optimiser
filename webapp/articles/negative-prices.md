Sometimes the price on Agile drops below zero. For those half hours, the more electricity you use, the more you're paid.

## Why it happens

Britain now has a lot of wind and solar power. On a windy, sunny day with low demand, perhaps a weekend afternoon or a stormy night, generators can produce more electricity than the country needs. Some of them can't easily switch off, so for a while the market pays people to use the extra power. Agile passes that price through to you.

It doesn't happen every day. It's most common on very windy days, and on sunny spring and summer weekends.

## How you'll know

Octopus publishes tomorrow's prices around 4pm. When any slot goes negative, this app's notification says so first, with the exact times.

## What to do in a negative slot

Use energy you'd have used anyway, just at a better time:

- Charge the **electric car** as much as it needs.
- Fill the **home battery**.
- Heat the **hot water tank** with the immersion heater.
- Run the **washing machine, dishwasher and tumble dryer**.
- Charge laptops, e-bikes and power banks.
- Batch-cook, if it suits you.

## What not to do

Don't waste energy just to earn a few pence, such as heating an empty house with the windows open. Negative prices are a sign of surplus clean power; the point is to use it instead of power at expensive times later.

## Why it matters beyond your bill

When homes soak up surplus wind and solar, less of that clean power is wasted and the grid needs fewer expensive fixes. Small shifts across many homes add up to something the energy system genuinely needs.
