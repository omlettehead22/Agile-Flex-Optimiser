A heat pump moves heat from the outside air (or the ground) into your home. Because it moves heat rather than making it, a good installation delivers around three units of heat for every unit of electricity it uses.

## The grant in England and Wales

The government's Boiler Upgrade Scheme gives homeowners in England and Wales:

- **£7,500** towards an air-to-water or ground source heat pump,
- **£2,500** towards an air-to-air heat pump,
- more for some homes replacing oil or LPG heating, for a limited period.

You don't claim it yourself: your **MCS-certified installer** applies, and the grant comes straight off your bill.

In **Scotland**, Home Energy Scotland offers its own grants and loans instead.

Grant levels and eligibility change, so check gov.uk or the Energy Saving Trust for the current rules before you get quotes.

## Running costs on smart tariffs

A heat pump uses electricity, so the tariff matters:

- **Time-of-use tariffs** can cut costs if the system heats the house and the hot water tank in cheaper hours, then coasts through the peak.
- **Agile** can work well, but avoid heating hard between 4pm and 7pm. Pre-warming the house a little before 4pm usually helps.
- Some suppliers have **tariffs designed for heat pumps** with cheaper slots spread through the day. Compare them.

## Getting a good installation

- Ask for a **room-by-room heat loss calculation**. Without one, the system may be the wrong size.
- Lower **flow temperatures** mean better efficiency. Some radiators may need upgrading.
- Ask what efficiency the installer expects (the seasonal performance) and get it in writing.

## Does it pay?

That depends on what you're replacing, your home's insulation and your tariff. Homes moving off oil, LPG or old electric heaters often see the biggest savings. Homes moving off mains gas may see smaller savings, but lower carbon emissions.

Check the grant first, get two or three quotes with a heat loss survey, and compare running costs on at least two tariffs.
