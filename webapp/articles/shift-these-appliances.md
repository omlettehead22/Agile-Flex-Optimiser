Timing only pays off for things that use a lot of energy and can wait. Moving a phone charger saves almost nothing. Moving the five below can make a real difference on a half-hourly tariff like Agile.

The figures are rough; your appliance's label or manual has the real numbers.

## 1. Electric car

An EV is the biggest flexible load most homes have. A top-up of 10 to 30 kWh is common, and the car is usually parked overnight, exactly when prices are low.

**How:** set a charging schedule in the car's app or on the charger. Many chargers can follow Agile prices automatically.

## 2. Tumble dryer

A full cycle in a vented or condenser dryer can use around 2 to 5 kWh, more than most other household appliances. Heat pump dryers use much less.

**How:** use the delay-start button, or dry on a line when the weather allows.

## 3. Immersion heater

A typical immersion heater draws about 3 kW, so an hour of heating uses about 3 kWh.

**How:** a timer or smart switch can heat the tank in the cheapest overnight hours, so you have hot water in the morning.

## 4. Dishwasher

Roughly 1 to 1.5 kWh a cycle, less on eco mode.

**How:** load it after dinner and set the delay so it runs in the night.

## 5. Washing machine

Around 0.5 to 1.5 kWh a wash, depending on the temperature. Washing at 30°C instead of 40°C cuts the energy used for most loads.

**How:** delay start, or run it in a cheap daytime slot if you're home.

## What it adds up to

On a typical day the gap between the peak and the cheapest hours is often 15p to 30p per kWh. Moving 5 kWh a day across that gap saves roughly 75p to £1.50 a day, which over a year can be a few hundred pounds. Move an EV as well and it can be much more.

## One rule

Don't run appliances unattended overnight if the manufacturer advises against it, and never cover a tumble dryer's lint filter. Saving money shouldn't come at the cost of safety.
